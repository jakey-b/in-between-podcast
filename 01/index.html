Edward Nolan is an Associate Professor of Classics in the Department of Foreign Languages and Literatures at National Taiwan University. He received a PhD in Classical Studies from the University of Michigan in 2021. A cultural historian and philologist, he researches ancient social thought, with a focus on ancient ideas about language. His current book project examines the Classical Greek historian Herodotus through a linguistic lens, revealing his engagement with other fifth-century BCE thinkers and his anticipation of modern linguistic concepts.
Laurent Cases is a historian of Late Antiquity, Late-Roman administration, institutions, and law, and currently an assistant professor of Classics in the Department of Foreign Languages and Literatures at the National Taiwan University, Taipei, Taiwan. He studied at the University of California, Santa Barbara (BA), the Institute for Medieval Studies at the University of Leeds (MA), and the Pennsylvania State University (MA/PhD), and was an ERC postdoctoral fellow in the faculty of Law at the University of Pavia. His work traces how the Roman Empire constructed its administrative apparatus and how this apparatus helped inform and transform cultural productions.
