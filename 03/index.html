Manuel Herrero-Puertas is an Associate Professor of Foreign Languages and Literatures at National Taiwan University, Taiwan, where he teaches courses on early and nineteenth-century American literature, disability studies, and the gothic. He writes on the intersection of literature, discourses of disability, neurodivergence, and horror.

His work has appeared in, among other venues, American Quarterly, Studies in American Fiction, Poe Studies, and the Journal of Literary & Cultural Disability Studies. His current monograph project argues for a non-psychoanalytic engagement with the transatlantic gothic, making a case instead for the genre’s accessible materiality and latent crip politics. 

Elizaveta Litovskaia is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Foreign Languages and Literatures at National Taiwan University, Taiwan. She teaches courses on Russian language and literature and graduate seminars on literature and fan reading practices on social media.

Her current research focuses on social media narratives about reading and literature, and on literature-related fandom practices in online communities. Previously, her work examined Russian and Soviet cookbooks from the nineteenth to the twenty-first centuries, exploring their connections to political, historical, and social contexts, as well as how these factors shape authors' self-presentation.
